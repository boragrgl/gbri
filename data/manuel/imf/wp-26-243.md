ABSTRACT: We develop a framework that links household balance sheets, bank solvency, and consumption 
to quantify the transmissi on of macroeconomic shocks and the effects of policy interventions . Using the 
Household Finance and Consumption Survey and supervisory bank-level data for 20 euro area countries, we 
project household default risk from observed arrears and introduce a cost- of-living-adjusted debt-service ratio. 
This measure outperforms the conventional debt-service ratio in predicting default in a statistical horse race , 
with delinquency risk more than doubling above a 70 percent threshold. Under a protrac ted Middle East war 
scenario, euro area households are resilient on average but highly heterogeneous: about one-third become 
overburdened, bank capital falls by roughly 125 basis points through higher household credit-loss provisions , 
and nearly 30 perc ent becom e consumers- at-risk, with a model-impli ed consumption- at-risk of about 5.4 
percent of aggregate consumption . Counterfactual policy experiments reveal important trade-offs . Narrowly 
targeted transfers minimiz e househol d distress per euro spent , partially targeted transfers produce the largest 
gains in bank resilience, and temporary interest rate reli ef for highly stretc hed borrowers can, under some 
conditions, mitigate bank capital losses. These results highlight the importance of targeting in the design of 
policies to support household welfare and financial stability.
